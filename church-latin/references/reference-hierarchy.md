# Reference Hierarchy — Which Lexicon to Use

The correct lexicon depends on the period and genre of the text. Consulting the wrong dictionary produces wrong translations. These are decision rules, not ranked preferences — a word in a 13th-century charter is not well served by Lewis & Short.

---

## Decision Rules by Period

### Patristic Latin (c.200–600)

**Primary:** Souter, A. *A Glossary of Later Latin to 600 A.D.* Clarendon Press, 1949.
- Compact but authoritative; specifically designed for Christian and late Latin; the standard for this period

**Secondary:** Blaise, Albert. *Dictionnaire latin-français des auteurs chrétiens*. Strasbourg: Le Latin Chrétien, 1954 [sometimes cited with Brepols as distributor].
- Comprehensive for Christian Latin vocabulary from the patristic period; includes ecclesiastical and theological senses; French definitions

**For biblical Latin:** Mohrmann, Christine. *Études sur le latin des chrétiens*. 4 vols. Edizioni di storia e letteratura, 1958–1977.
- Not a lexicon but the foundational scholarly analysis of Christian Latin registers and semantic shifts; essential for understanding *why* words mean what they mean

**For individual authors:** Author-specific glossaries exist for Augustine, Jerome, and others; prefer these when the author is identified

### Medieval Latin — General (c.600–1500)

**Primary (Continental):** Niermeyer, J.F. *Mediae Latinitatis Lexicon Minus*. Brill, 1976; revised edition 2002.
- Best single-volume dictionary for medieval Latin documents, charters, and administrative texts; essential for institutional and legal vocabulary; Dutch/Latin definitions with Latin equivalents

**For quick British/Irish reference:** Latham, R.E. *Revised Medieval Latin Word-List from British and Irish Sources*. British Academy / OUP, 1965. Compact single-volume desk reference; useful for quick lookups; largely superseded by DMLBS for serious scholarly work but remains widely used.

**For orientation and bibliography:** Mantello, F.A.C., and Rigg, A.G. (eds.). *Medieval Latin: An Introduction and Bibliographical Guide*. CUA Press, 1996.
- Comprehensive survey of medieval Latin genres, authors, and scholarship; the standard English-language introduction; essential for establishing context and finding specialist bibliography

**Primary (British Isles):** DMLBS — *Dictionary of Medieval Latin from British Sources*. Oxford University Press / British Academy, 1975–2013. 3 vols.
- The standard for Latin from Britain; also useful for Irish Latin when DMLCS is unavailable

**Primary (Ireland / Insular):** DMLCS — *Dictionary of Medieval Latin from Celtic Sources*. Royal Irish Academy, 2019–.
- The only lexicon specifically designed for Hiberno-Latin and Celtic Latin; essential for any text of Irish provenance; covers distinctly Insular meanings not found in Continental dictionaries
- Available online through Royal Irish Academy

**Secondary (Medieval Christian & Ecclesiastical):** Blaise, Albert. *Dictionnaire latin-français des auteurs du Moyen Âge*. Turnhout: Brepols, 1975 (Latin title: *Lexicon Latinitatis Medii Aevi*).
- The standard reference for medieval Christian vocabulary, liturgy, and theology after the Merovingian era. Distinct from Blaise's 1954 Patristic dictionary.

**Secondary (Scholarly/Comprehensive):** NGML — *Novum Glossarium Mediae Latinitatis*. Copenhagen: Ejnar Munksgaard / Union Académique Internationale, 1957–.
- Major international collaborative dictionary covering European medieval Latin from 800 to 1200; highly detailed semantic histories.

**For classical legacy terms:** Lewis, C.T., and Short, C. *A Latin Dictionary*. Clarendon Press, 1879 (standard edition).
- Use only as a check on classical meanings; do not allow it to override ecclesiastical senses

### Neo-Latin / Renaissance (c.1450–1600)

**Primary:** IJsewijn, J., and Sacré, D. *Companion to Neo-Latin Studies*. 2 vols. Leuven University Press, 1990–1998.
- Cite the Leuven University Press edition (vol. 1, 1990; vol. 2, 1998), not the 1977 North-Holland single-volume edition (IJsewijn alone); the two editions differ significantly in coverage
- Not a dictionary but the standard survey of Neo-Latin genres, authors, and conventions; essential orientation

**Secondary for classical vocabulary:** OLD — *Oxford Latin Dictionary*. Clarendon Press, 1968–1982; 2nd ed. 2012.
- More rigorous for classical usage than Lewis & Short; use for humanist Latin where classical meanings are specifically intended
- Does not cover medieval or ecclesiastical vocabulary

**For humanist correspondence and diplomacy:** Tunberg, T., and various specialized studies; consult on a case-by-case basis

### Early Modern (c.1600–1800)

**Primary:** Collins, J.F. *A Primer of Ecclesiastical Latin*. CUA Press, 1985.
- Practical grammatical and lexical guide focused on post-Tridentine ecclesiastical and liturgical Latin; excellent coverage of the Missal, Breviary, and curial documents

**Secondary:** Stelten, Leo F. *Dictionary of Ecclesiastical Latin*. Hendrickson Publishers, 1995.
- Compact; focused on Missal, Breviary, and modern Church documents; English glosses with specialized ecclesiastical senses; useful quick reference

**German ecclesiastical Latin reference:** Sleumer, Albert. *Kirchenlateinisches Wörterbuch*. Limburg: Steffen, 1926; reprinted Hildesheim: Olms.
- Comprehensive German-language dictionary of ecclesiastical Latin covering all periods from patristic through modern; strong on liturgical, canonical, and curial vocabulary; useful for terms not in Stelten or Collins and for verifying sense against a wider ecclesiastical canon

**For Jesuit Latin:** IJsewijn & Sacré as above; no dedicated Jesuit Latin monograph exists

### Modern (c.1800–1960)

**Primary for neologisms:** *Lexicon Recentis Latinitatis*. Vatican Latinitas Foundation, 1992; revised 2003.
- Official Vatican reference for modern concepts expressed in Latin (*aeroplanum*, *telephonicum*, *televisio*); essential for any 20th-century Vatican text

**For 1917 CIC:** Peters, Edward N. *The 1917 Pio-Benedictine Code of Canon Law in English Translation with Extensive Scholarly Apparatus*. Ignatius Press, 2001.
- De facto standard English translation of the 1917 Code; use this as the reference translation for any CIC 1917 canon

**For encyclicals and papal documents:** Latin text in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* (from 1909) is authoritative; compare with standard translations published by the Holy See or Vatican website

## Decision Rules by Genre

### Hagiographic Texts
Niermeyer for administrative vocabulary; DMLBS or DMLCS for Insular hagiography; Souter for patristic saints' lives; Blaise (1954 or 1975) for Christian vocabulary throughout

### Scholastic Texts
For Aquinas and 13th-century scholasticism: Roy J. Deferrari et al., *A Lexicon of St. Thomas Aquinas* (Catholic University of America Press, 1949) is the specialized reference. For general scholastic vocabulary, use Niermeyer with care; for individual distinctions (*esse*, *quidditas*, *suppositum*), the original author's usage in context takes precedence over any general lexicon

### Canonical and Legal Documents
Niermeyer first; then DMLBS/DMLCS for Insular provenance; Peters for 1917 CIC. For pre-Tridentine canon law (Decretum, Decretales): specialized canonical dictionaries; Niermeyer covers much of this

### Inscriptions and Epigraphy
For classical formulae surviving into medieval use: *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* (CIL) conventions; for Christian epigraphy: *Inscriptiones Latinae Christianae Veteres* (Diehl) for late antique; Du Cange for later medieval

### Liturgical Texts
Collins and Stelten for standard liturgical vocabulary; Mohrmann for deep semantic analysis of liturgical language

## Abbreviation Resources

For manuscript and document abbreviations:
- **Cappelli** — Cappelli, A. *Dizionario di abbreviature latine ed italiane*. Milan, 1899; numerous reprints. The standard abbreviation dictionary; covers Insular, Caroline, Gothic, and humanist hands. Essential for any abbreviated manuscript. Available in partial digitized form.
- **Abbreviationes Online** — Olaf Pluta's electronic database of medieval Latin abbreviations. The de facto standard digital tool for manuscript and palaeographical abbreviation lookups.

For charter formulae:
- Giry, A. *Manuel de diplomatique*. Paris, 1894 — standard for western European documentary formulas
