# False Friends and Extended Glossary — Ecclesiastical Latin

## Top 20 Danger List — Full Context

These twenty terms produce material errors when translated by classical default. The "consequence" column explains why the error matters, not just what it is.

| # | Latin | Classical default | Correct ecclesiastical sense | Consequence of error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | religio | religion, piety, superstition | a religious order or congregation (*ingredi religionem* = "to enter an order") | Implies interreligious division where the text is about internal Catholic governance |
| 2 | conversatio morum | conversation of manners (verbal or social exchange) | the formal third Benedictine vow: the ongoing reorientation of one's entire manner of living toward God — never a phrase about verbal conduct | A researcher treats a formal canonical vow of monastic consecration as a generic exhortation about polite behaviour |
| 3 | capitulum | small head, chapter of a book | the Chapter (governing body of a cathedral, diocese, or religious house/province) | Chapter acts appear to be book editing notes rather than official governance records |
| 4 | princeps | prince, emperor, secular ruler | abbot or head of a monastic federation (early medieval Irish/monastic texts) | Historians search for secular royalty where the text refers to a monastic superior |
| 5 | civitas | city, state, citizenship | monastery or monastic town (early medieval and Insular contexts) | Misidentifies the geography of early Christian Ireland, where cities did not exist outside major monastic centres |
| 6 | definitor | one who defines or limits | provincial or general councilor (assistant on the governing council of an order) | Suggests a lexicographical or dogmatic role rather than a crucial administrative office |
| 7 | regularis | standard, normal, linear | regular clergy (living under a religious rule, as opposed to secular clergy) | Misrepresents the fundamental canonical distinction between order-based and diocesan clergy |
| 8 | beneficium | benefit, favor, kindness | benefice (an ecclesiastical office endowed with permanent revenue) | Obscures the legal reality of a formal appointment carrying specific property rights and assets |
| 9 | oratio | speech, public oration, rhetoric | prayer or liturgical collect | Turns a priest leading liturgical prayers into a secular politician giving a speech |
| 10 | vicarius | substitute, proxy, underling | Vicar (an official deputy exercising ordinary or delegated canonical power) | Makes a powerful diocesan legal officer (Vicar General) look like a temporary replacement worker |
| 11 | erectio | setting upright, raising up | canonical erection (the formal legal decree establishing a community, altar, or benefice) | Suggests physical building construction rather than the formal canonical decree establishing a corporate legal identity |
| 12 | parochia | diocese (late Latin) | 1. Parish (post-medieval); 2. Monastic federation (early medieval) | In early medieval texts, creates geographical anachronisms; in modern texts, may be mistaken for diocese |
| 13 | exhibitor | showman, presenter, producer | petitioner or presenter of a canonical document (in episcopal certificates) | Makes the petitioner of a legal certificate look like an entertainer |
| 14 | dimissoriales | dismissal letters, letters of rejection | dimissorial letters (episcopal authorization to be ordained by another bishop) | Implies the cleric was dismissed or expelled rather than given a routine ordination licence |
| 15 | conversus | turned around, converted | lay brother (a monastic member dedicated to manual labour under a simplified rule) | Implies the monk was a convert from another religion rather than identifying his specific monastic class |
| 16 | postulatio | legal demand, request, claim | postulation (petitioning a superior to appoint an impeded candidate to an elected office) | Hides the legal reality of a canonical election petition, rendering the governance dispute unintelligible |
| 17 | cura animarum | care of minds/souls (literary) | cure of souls (the official legal pastoral responsibility of a parish priest) | Turns a legal sacramental diocesan assignment into generic psychology or counselling |
| 18 | saeculum | century, generation, age | the world / secular life (life outside the cloister) | *revertit ad saeculum* rendered as "he returned to the century" or "returned to the age" is unintelligible; it means he left his vows |
| 19 | lector | reader | professor of theology or philosophy in a house of studies; OR the minor order of Lector | Makes a senior academic theologian teaching seminary courses look like someone hired to read texts aloud |
| 20 | rescriptum | written reply of an emperor | rescript (written response of a pope or congregation granting a favor or legal decision) | Implies secular imperial control over canon law, misrepresenting the source and authority of the document |

## Extended Glossary

Additional terms requiring careful translation in ecclesiastical contexts. Organized by category.

### Religious Life and Vows

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| professio / professus | religious profession / one who has made profession | *professio* = the formal act of taking religious vows; *professus* = a professed religious; not "profession" in the career sense |
| votum / vota | religious vow / vows | *vota solemnia* = solemn vows; *vota simplicia* = simple vows; the canonical distinction is legally significant |
| novitiatus | novitiate | the period of formation before profession; or the place where novices live |
| novitius | novice | a person in the novitiate, not yet professed |
| postulans | postulant | candidate accepted for admission to the novitiate, not yet received the habit |
| exclaustratio | exclaustration | canonically authorized temporary absence from the monastery; not "expulsion" |
| apostasia a religione | apostasy from religious life | canonical term for a professed religious who unlawfully abandons the institute; not generic apostasy from faith |
| habitus religionis | religious habit | the distinctive clothing of a religious order; not "habit" in the psychological sense |
| chorista / choralis | choir monk/sister | a fully professed religious who participates in the Divine Office; distinguished from lay brother/sister |
| frater laicus | lay brother | a religious who takes vows but is not ordained and participates in manual work rather than the choir; also *frater conversus* |
| conversatio | conversation, manner | the entire pattern of monastic life directed toward God; in the Benedictine vow *conversatio morum* (conversion of manners), the ongoing reorientation of one's whole manner of living — never a verbal exchange |

### Offices and Authority

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| praelatus | prelate | a superior with jurisdiction (bishop, abbot, superior general); not a generic dignitary |
| praelatura | prelature | the office, dignity, or jurisdiction of a prelate |
| ordinarius | ordinary | an ecclesiastical superior with ordinary (inherent, not delegated) jurisdiction: bishop in a diocese, superior general in a religious order |
| provincialis | provincial superior / provincial | the superior of a religious province; not a noun meaning "provincial person" |
| definitorium | council of definitors | the governing council of a religious province or general chapter |
| secretarius | secretary | in a religious order context: a senior official, not a clerical worker |
| syndicus | syndic | legal and financial agent of a monastery or religious community; not a "synodist" |
| praefectus | prefect | director of a specific function (Prefect of Studies, Prefect of Postulants); not "mayor" |
| economus | bursar / procurator | the financial administrator of a house or province |
| procurator | procurator | legal and/or financial representative; distinct from *economus* in some orders |

### Sacramental and Liturgical

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| absolutio | absolution | in sacramental context: the juridical act of the priest in the sacrament of penance; not mere "forgiveness" |
| paenitentia | penance | the sacrament; or the canonical penalty; or the virtue; context determines which |
| unctio | anointing | *Extrema Unctio* = Extreme Unction (Last Rites / Anointing of the Sick) |
| viaticum | Viaticum | the Eucharist given to the dying; not generic "provisions for a journey" |
| communio | Holy Communion / the Eucharist | not merely "community" or "union" |
| reconciliatio | reconciliation | the sacramental restoration to grace after mortal sin; or a formal ecclesiastical reconciliation ceremony |
| ordinatio | ordination | the sacrament of Holy Orders; not "ordering" or "arrangement" |
| missio / dimissio | mission / dismissal | context-dependent; *missio* may be a canonical missionary assignment; *dimissio* may be formal release from religious life |

### Documents and Procedures

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| litteras testimoniales | letters testimonial | certificate attesting a fact (ordination, good standing); not generic "testimony" |
| litterae commendaticiae | letters of commendation | introducing a cleric or religious to another community or superior |
| indultum | indult | a special faculty or privilege granted by the Holy See; not "indulgence" |
| dispensatio | dispensation | the relaxation of a canonical law in a specific case; not "arrangement" or "distribution" |
| declaratio | declaration | a formal authoritative interpretation of existing law; not a general announcement |
| monitio | monition | a canonical warning (three required before certain penalties); not a generic "warning" |
| canon / canones | canon / canons | individual legislative provisions; or the Roman Canon of the Mass; context determines |
| acta | acts | official record of proceedings (Chapter acts, conciliar acts); not general "actions" |
| processus | process | a canonical judicial or administrative procedure; not a manufacturing process |
| causa | cause / case | in canonical usage: a judicial case being heard; *causa canonizationis* = the cause for canonization |

### Monastic and Community Life

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| domus | religious house | a canonically established community; not merely "house" (physical building) |
| domus formata | formed house | a house with the canonical minimum number of members to function independently |
| domus non formata | unformed house | a canonical dependent community below the minimum |
| locus | house, place, site | in religious order usage: a house of the order; *locum habere* = to have a house (of the order) |
| solitudo | retreat / hermitage | context-dependent; in some orders (Passionist): a monastery; in others: a place of solitary prayer |
| collatio | conference / reading | the evening reading and conference from the *Collationes* of Cassian; extended to any community spiritual reading or conference |
| capitulum culparum | chapter of faults | the regular meeting at which religious publicly acknowledge their faults before the community |
| refectorium | refectory | the community dining room; not "rectory" |
| claustrum | cloister | the enclosed walkway surrounding the garth; by extension, the enclosure of the monastery |
| parlatorium | parlour | the room for receiving visitors; not "parliament" |
| conventus | assembly, meeting, association | in Mendicant orders (Dominican, Franciscan): a religious house and its community — the building, or the community living there; *conventus magnus* = a major house of the order; do not render as "assembly" or "convention" in these contexts |

### Persons and Status

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| sodalis | confrere / member | a fellow member of a religious community or congregation; not "soul" or "soldier" |
| alumnus | student, pupil | in a seminary or house of studies; not an "alumni" in the modern graduation sense |
| clericus | cleric | any person in Holy Orders or, in canon law, any person who has received tonsure (pre-1983); not a generic office worker |
| saecularis | secular (clergy) | diocesan clergy not under a religious rule; opposed to *regularis*; not "secular" in the modern cultural sense |
| eremita | hermit | a person living the solitary life under Church approval; not a generic recluse |
| oblatus | oblate | a person associated with a monastery through formal oblation, not a full member; or in some orders, a clerical candidate |
| supplex | petitioner | one who humbly petitions in a canonical process; not merely "supplicant" in a devotional sense |
| exhibitor praesentium | the bearer/presenter of the present document | the person physically presenting this document; a technical formula in episcopal certificates |

### Temporal and Administrative

| Latin | Correct rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| annus gratiae | year of grace | *Anno Gratiae MDCCC* = "In the year of grace 1800" — a dating formula, not a description |
| annus Domini | year of the Lord | standard AD dating formula |
| terminus | limit, deadline | canonical *terminus* = the set date by which an obligation must be fulfilled; not "terminus" in the transport sense |
| praescriptio | prescription | in canon law: the acquisition or loss of a right through the passage of time; not "prescription" in the medical sense |
| usus | use, usage | *usus fructus* = usufruct (right to use and enjoy property); *usus iuris* = legal usage |
| fructus | revenues, income, produce | in ecclesiastical administration: the revenues from a benefice or estate; not "fruit" |
| visitatio | visitation | the formal canonical inspection of a religious house or diocese by a superior; not a social visit |
| visitator | visitor | the official conducting a canonical visitation; not a tourist |
| praescriptum | prescription, regulation | a specific rule or directive; not a medical document |
| reformatio | the (Protestant) Reformation | in Catholic curial, conciliar, and religious order documents of c.1520–1650: **internal reform of the Church** — restoration of discipline, clerical standards, liturgical regularity. The Tridentine *Decreta de reformatione* are about Catholic institutional reform, not the Protestant Reformation. Translating *reformatio* as "the Reformation" in a Catholic document inverts the text's meaning |

### Patristic and Biblical Vocabulary

| Latin | Wrong default | Correct sense |
|---|---|---|
| *hebraica veritas* | "Hebrew truth" (general) | Jerome's technical term for the original Hebrew text of the Old Testament, as opposed to the Septuagint (Greek translation); not a generic claim about truth but a text-critical position: Jerome privileged the Hebrew Masoretic text over the LXX as the base for the Vulgate. Using "Hebrew truth" as a literal translation obscures the text-critical argument |
| *discretio* | discretion, caution, tact | in Gregory the Great and the monastic tradition: **spiritual discernment** — the capacity to judge the interior states of souls and apply appropriate pastoral remedies; a theological virtue, not merely a social one. *Discretio spirituum* = discernment of spirits |
