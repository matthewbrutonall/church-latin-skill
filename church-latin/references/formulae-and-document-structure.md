# Formulae and Document Structure — Charters, Bulls, and Notarial Acts

A charter, papal bull, episcopal act, or notarial instrument is not free prose. It is a **formulary**
built from named, ordered parts, each carrying legal weight, and each filled with stock phrases whose
meaning is idiomatic and fixed. The single most common error in translating diplomatic Latin is
rendering these formulae word-for-word: *servus servorum Dei* is not "slave of the slaves of God" in
tone, *Noverint universi* is not "let everyone have known," and *ad perpetuam rei memoriam* is a
technical heading, not a sentence about memory. Identify the part, recognise the formula, render it by
its established English equivalent.

The science of these parts is **diplomatics**; its three-part skeleton is the *protocol*, the *text*
(*textus* / *corpus*), and the *eschatocol*. Parse the document into these before translating.

---

## 1. The three-part skeleton

| Part | Latin | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Protocol** | *protocollum* | Opening frame: who speaks, to whom, with what greeting |
| **Text** | *textus / corpus* | The substance: preamble, statement, the act itself, sanctions, authentication |
| **Eschatocol** | *eschatocollum* | Closing frame: date, place, signatures, final formula |

## 2. The Protocol (opening frame)

| Element | Latin | What it is | Idiomatic rendering |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Invocatio** | *invocatio* | Calling on God at the very head | *In nomine Domini* → "In the name of the Lord"; *In nomine sanctae et individuae Trinitatis* → "In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity" |
| **Intitulatio** | *intitulatio* | Name and title of the issuer | *N., Dei gratia episcopus …* → "N., by the grace of God bishop of …" |
| **Inscriptio** | *inscriptio* | The addressee(s) | *dilectis filiis …* → "to his beloved sons …"; *universis Christi fidelibus* → "to all the faithful of Christ" |
| **Salutatio** | *salutatio* | The greeting | *salutem in Domino sempiternam* → "eternal salvation in the Lord"; *salutem et apostolicam benedictionem* → "greeting and apostolic blessing" |

The invocation may be **verbal** (words, as above) or **symbolic** (a chrismon ☧, a cross, the
*Rota* of a papal bull) — note a symbolic invocation, do not try to translate the sign.

## 3. The Text (*textus*)

| Element | Latin | What it is | Idiomatic rendering / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arenga** (preamble) | *arenga / prooemium* | General pious or moral reflection motivating the act | Often a rolling sentence on the frailty of memory or the duty of charity; render its sense, do not flatten to a cliché. Frequently the source of Vulgate allusion — scan it |
| **Notificatio** (publication) | *notificatio / promulgatio* | "Be it known" clause introducing the act | *Noverint universi …* → "Let all men know …"; *Notum sit omnibus …* → "Be it known to all …" |
| **Narratio** | *narratio* | The background/circumstances leading to the act | The petition, the dispute, the facts recited as grounds |
| **Dispositio** | *dispositio* | **The operative act itself** — the grant, confirmation, judgement | The legal core; performative verbs (*damus, concedimus, confirmamus, statuimus, ordinamus*). Translate the verb's force exactly — a grant is not a confirmation |
| **Sanctio** | *sanctio* | Penalty/blessing clause protecting the act | Prohibitive + comminatory (curse on violators) and often a benedictory (blessing on observers) half; see §4 |
| **Corroboratio** | *corroboratio* | Statement of the means of authentication | "In witness whereof I have set my seal" — announces seal, witnesses, or sign manual to come |

## 4. The Eschatocol (closing frame)

| Element | Latin | What it is | Idiomatic rendering / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Datatio** (date) | *datatio* | Place and time | *Datum* / *Actum* + place + date. *Datum per manum …* = "Given by the hand of …" (the chancery officer). Resolve the date with `dating-system.md` |
| **Subscriptions** | *subscriptiones* | Signatures of issuer, witnesses, notary | *Ego N. subscripsi* → "I, N., have subscribed"; *testes* → "witnesses"; the notary's *signum* |
| **Apprecatio** | *apprecatio* | Closing devout word | *Amen*; *Feliciter*; *In Dei nomine feliciter* → keep "Amen" / render "Happily, in the name of God" |

**Sanctio detail.** The penalty clause often has two matching halves:
- *Comminatory / anathema:* *Si quis autem … contraire praesumpserit, indignationem omnipotentis Dei
  et beatorum Petri et Pauli apostolorum eius se noverit incursurum* → "But if anyone shall presume to
  go against this …, let him know that he will incur the wrath of Almighty God and of his blessed
  apostles Peter and Paul." Render *anathema sit* as "let him be anathema" (a formal canonical
  excommunication-curse — do not soften to "let him be cursed").
- *Benedictory:* *Cunctis autem … iura sua servantibus sit pax Domini nostri Iesu Christi* →
  "But to all who preserve its rights, may the peace of our Lord Jesus Christ be …"

## 5. High-frequency stock phrases

Render idiomatically, not word-for-word. These have established English equivalents.

| Latin formula | Where | Idiomatic English |
|---|---|---|
| *In nomine Domini, amen* | invocatio | "In the name of the Lord, amen" |
| *In nomine sanctae et individuae Trinitatis* | invocatio | "In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity" |
| *N., Dei gratia …* | intitulatio | "N., by the grace of God …" |
| *N. episcopus, servus servorum Dei* | intitulatio (papal) | "N., bishop, servant of the servants of God" (the pope's formal style — an idiom of humility, keep the fixed phrase) |
| *dilecto filio / dilectis filiis* | inscriptio | "to his beloved son / sons" |
| *Universis praesentes litteras inspecturis, salutem in Domino sempiternam* | inscriptio+salutatio | "To all who shall inspect the present letters, eternal salvation in the Lord" |
| *salutem et apostolicam benedictionem* | salutatio (papal) | "greeting and apostolic blessing" |
| *Ad perpetuam rei memoriam* | (heading of a bull) | "For the perpetual remembrance of the matter" — a formal opening formula of solemn bulls, functioning as a heading; do **not** parse as a purpose clause of a preceding sentence |
| *Ad futuram rei memoriam* | heading | "For the future remembrance of the matter" |
| *Noverint universi praesentes et futuri (quod)* | notificatio | "Let all, present and to come, know (that)" |
| *Notum sit omnibus (tam praesentibus quam futuris)* | notificatio | "Be it known to all (both present and to come)" |
| *Sciant praesentes et futuri (quod)* | notificatio | "Let those present and to come know (that)" |
| *damus, concedimus et confirmamus* | dispositio | "we give, grant, and confirm" (keep the three distinct verbs) |
| *In cuius rei testimonium* | corroboratio | "In witness whereof" |
| *In cuius rei testimonium sigillum nostrum praesentibus apponi fecimus / duximus apponendum* | corroboratio | "In witness whereof we have caused our seal to be affixed to these presents" |
| *praesentes litteras / has litteras nostras* | throughout | "the present letters / these our letters" (a legal instrument, not a personal letter) |
| *Datum (Actum) apud N., anno Domini …* | datatio | "Given (Done) at N., in the year of the Lord …" |
| *Datum per manum N.* | datatio | "Given by the hand of N." |
| *Si quis autem … anathema sit* | sanctio | "But if anyone …, let him be anathema" |
| *In Dei nomine, amen* / *Feliciter* | apprecatio | "In the name of God, amen" / "Happily" |

**Notarial acts** add their own frame: *In nomine Domini amen. Anno … indictione … die …* opening,
and close with the notary's authentication: *Et ego N., publicus … notarius, praedictis omnibus …
interfui et … in hanc publicam formam redegi, signoque meo solito signavi rogatus* → "And I, N., a
public notary, was present at all the aforesaid … and reduced them into this public form, and, being
asked, signed with my customary sign." Keep *rogatus* ("being asked/requested") — it is the technical
term for the party's request that made the act official.

---

## False-friend caution

Diplomatic formulae are **fixed idioms**; translating them literally corrupts the sense:

- *servus servorum Dei* — the papal style "servant of the servants of God," not a statement about
  slavery.
- *litterae / praesentes litterae* — "letters" here means a **legal instrument / charter**, not
  correspondence; *litterae patentes* = letters patent (open), *litterae clausae* = letters close
  (sealed shut).
- *Ad perpetuam rei memoriam* — a solemn-bull heading formula, not a clause meaning "so that the thing
  may be remembered forever" hanging off a verb.
- *Noverint universi* — perfect-subjunctive "let all know," not "everyone will have known."
- *Datum per manum* — "given by the hand of" the dating-chancellor; it names the officer who dated the
  act, not necessarily the scribe.
- *actum* vs *datum* — *actum* stresses where the act was **done/transacted**; *datum* where/when it
  was **issued/dated**. In a single document they can differ; note it.
- *anathema* — a formal canonical curse (excommunication), not a generic "curse" or "abomination."

## Worked example — the opening of a solemn bull

**Innocentius episcopus, servus servorum Dei, dilectis filiis abbati et conventui monasterii S.
Mariae, salutem et apostolicam benedictionem. Cum a nobis petitur quod iustum est et honestum, tam
vigor aequitatis quam ordo exigit rationis ut id per sollicitudinem officii nostri ad debitum
perducatur effectum.**

Parse:
- *Innocentius episcopus, servus servorum Dei* — **intitulatio**: "Innocent, bishop, servant of the
  servants of God" (the pope; identify which Innocent from context/date, not from the word alone)
- *dilectis filiis abbati et conventui monasterii S. Mariae* — **inscriptio**: "to his beloved sons
  the abbot and convent of the monastery of St Mary" (*conventui* = the community, not "a convention")
- *salutem et apostolicam benedictionem* — **salutatio**: "greeting and apostolic blessing"
- *Cum a nobis petitur quod iustum est et honestum … ut id … ad debitum perducatur effectum* —
  **arenga**: the stock preamble of confirmation bulls — "When something just and honest is asked of
  us, both the force of equity and the order of reason demand that, through the diligence of our
  office, it be carried to its due effect."

→ **"Innocent, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to his beloved sons the abbot and convent of
the monastery of St Mary, greeting and apostolic blessing. When something just and honourable is
asked of us, both the force of equity and the order of reason require that, through the solicitude of
our office, it be brought to its due effect."**

*Note:* the sentence beginning *Cum a nobis petitur* is a recognised standard *arenga* (the
"*Cum a nobis*" preamble) reused across countless confirmation bulls — recognising it as formula
prevents over-reading it as bespoke reasoning about this specific monastery.
