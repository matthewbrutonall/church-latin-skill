# Psalm Numbering and Vulgate Versification

Ecclesiastical texts cite Scripture by **Vulgate** reference, and the Vulgate's psalm numbers, verse
numbers, and book names do **not** match a modern Hebrew-based Protestant or critical edition. A
liturgical rubric, a papal bull, a monastic customary, or a patristic commentary that cites "Ps. xxi"
means the psalm that modern English Bibles print as **Psalm 22** — the *Deus, Deus meus, quare me
dereliquisti* ("My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me"). Resolve the reference in the Vulgate
system first, then, if a modern reader needs it, give the modern equivalent in brackets. Never assume
a cited chapter-and-verse matches a modern Bible.

**Default rule: ecclesiastical Latin texts use Vulgate/Septuagint numbering.** The Douay-Rheims
Bible follows the Vulgate exactly, which is why it is this skill's scriptural anchor — a Vulgate
citation maps one-to-one onto Douay-Rheims.

---

## 1. Why the numbers diverge

The Latin Psalter (both the *Gallicanum* of the Vulgate and the older Latin Psalters) follows the
**Greek Septuagint (LXX)** division of the psalms; modern Hebrew-based Bibles follow the **Masoretic**
division. The two traditions divide and join the same 150 psalms at different points, so across most
of the Psalter the Vulgate number is **one lower** than the Hebrew/modern number.

## 2. The psalm-numbering map

| Hebrew / modern | Vulgate / Septuagint / Douay | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| 1 – 8 | 1 – 8 | **identical** |
| 9 – 10 | **9** | Vulgate joins Hebrew 9 + 10 into one psalm |
| 11 – 113 | **10 – 112** | Vulgate = modern **− 1** (the large middle range) |
| 114 – 115 | **113** | Vulgate joins Hebrew 114 + 115 |
| 116 | **114 – 115** | Vulgate splits Hebrew 116 into two |
| 117 – 146 | **116 – 145** | Vulgate = modern **− 1** again |
| 147 | **146 – 147** | Vulgate splits Hebrew 147 into two |
| 148 – 150 | 148 – 150 | **identical** |

**Practical rules for conversion:**
- Psalms **1–8** and **148–150**: the numbers are the same in both systems.
- Psalms **Vulgate 10–112** and **Vulgate 116–145**: add 1 to get the modern number
  (Vulgate 22 → modern 23; Vulgate 50 → modern 51 the *Miserere*; Vulgate 109 → modern 110).
- At the four join/split points (Vulgate 9, 113, 114–115, 146–147) the mapping is not a simple ±1 —
  use the table.

The most-cited example: **Vulgate Psalm 22 = modern Psalm 23**, *Dominus regit me et nihil mihi
deerit* — "The Lord ruleth me: and I shall want nothing" (Douay). The penitential *Miserere* is
**Vulgate 50 = modern 51**; the messianic *Dixit Dominus* is **Vulgate 109 = modern 110**.

## 3. Verse numbering — the superscription is counted

Within a psalm, the Vulgate frequently **numbers the Hebrew superscription (title) as verse 1** (or
folds it into verse 1). Modern editions that leave the title unnumbered therefore run one verse
*lower* than the Vulgate for the body of such a psalm. When a Latin source cites a specific verse of a
titled psalm, expect a possible one-verse offset against a modern edition, and check the *text* (the
opening words) rather than trusting the bare number. Cite by opening words (*incipit*) when precision
matters — e.g. "Ps. 50[51]:3, *Miserere mei Deus*."

## 4. Vulgate book names

The Vulgate (and Douay-Rheims after it) names and divides several books differently from modern
Protestant Bibles. Recognise the Latin book name and give the modern equivalent when helpful:

| Vulgate / Douay Latin | Vulgate book | Modern (Protestant) name |
|---|---|---|
| *Regum I–IV* (1–4 Kings) | 1–4 Kings | **1–2 Samuel + 1–2 Kings** (1 Reg = 1 Sam, 2 Reg = 2 Sam, 3 Reg = 1 Kgs, 4 Reg = 2 Kgs) |
| *Paralipomenon I–II* | 1–2 Paralipomenon | **1–2 Chronicles** |
| *Esdrae I–II* | 1–2 Esdras | **Ezra (1 Esdras) + Nehemiah (2 Esdras / *Nehemias*)** |
| *Canticum Canticorum* | Canticle of Canticles | **Song of Songs / Song of Solomon** |
| *Ecclesiastes* | Ecclesiastes | Ecclesiastes |
| *Ecclesiasticus* / *Sirach* | Ecclesiasticus | **Sirach** (a deuterocanonical book; *not* Ecclesiastes) |
| *Sapientia* (*Liber Sapientiae*) | Wisdom | Wisdom of Solomon (deuterocanonical) |
| *Threni* / *Lamentationes* | Lamentations | Lamentations |
| *Isaias, Ieremias, Ezechiel, Osee, Michaeas, Sophonias, Aggaeus, Zacharias, Malachias* | prophets | Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Hosea, Micah, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi |
| *Apocalypsis* | Apocalypse | **Revelation** |

**Cautions:**
- *Regum* / "Kings" in a Vulgate citation covers **Samuel and Kings together** — "2 Kings" (2 Reg) in
  a patristic or liturgical text is modern **2 Samuel**, not modern 2 Kings.
- *Ecclesiasticus* (Sirach) is a different book from *Ecclesiastes* — a single-letter slip that
  changes the book. Keep them distinct.
- *Esdras* numbering is a minefield: within the Vulgate itself 1 Esdras = Ezra and 2 Esdras =
  Nehemiah, while some editions and the Vulgate appendix add *3 and 4 Esdras* (apocryphal). Identify
  which enumeration is in play.
- *Apocalypsis* is the last book — "Revelation" — never render it as a generic "revelation/disclosure"
  when it is a book citation.

## 5. Which system is the source using?

- **Any ecclesiastical Latin text** — liturgy, Breviary, Missal, canon law, patristics, monastic
  rules, papal and episcopal documents — cites the **Vulgate** numbering by default. Resolve in
  Vulgate, anchor to Douay-Rheims.
- A modern scholarly edition or a text influenced by Hebrew scholarship may use Masoretic numbers;
  when a citation's opening words don't match the Vulgate psalm of that number, suspect this and check.
- When you give a modern reader the equivalent, use the **dual form**: *Ps. 22[23]* (Vulgate[modern]),
  so neither tradition is lost.

---

## Worked example — resolving a "Ps. xxi" citation

A monastic sermon reads: **Sicut scriptum est in psalmo xxi: "Deus, Deus meus, respice in me, quare
me dereliquisti?"**

1. **Read the numeral:** *xxi* = 21 (Vulgate numbering, as expected in a monastic text).
2. **Locate in the map (§2):** Vulgate 21 falls in the range Vulgate 10–112, so modern = Vulgate + 1
   → **modern Psalm 22**.
3. **Confirm by the *incipit*:** the quoted words *Deus, Deus meus … quare me dereliquisti* are the
   opening of that psalm — the cry Christ speaks from the Cross — confirming Vulgate 21 = modern 22,
   not the *Dominus regit me* (which is Vulgate 22 = modern 23). This *incipit* check is why numeral
   alone is unsafe: Vulgate 21 and Vulgate 22 are adjacent and easily transposed.
4. **Anchor to Douay-Rheims** for the English: Ps 21(22):2 — *"O God, my God, look upon me: why hast
   thou forsaken me?"*
5. **Present with the dual reference:** "as it is written in Psalm 21[22]: *O God, my God, look upon
   me; why hast thou forsaken me?*"

→ **"As it is written in Psalm 21[22]: 'O God, my God, look upon me: why hast thou forsaken me?'"**

*Note:* had the citation been carried over naively as "Psalm 21" against a modern Bible, the reader
would have been sent to the wrong psalm (modern 21, *Deus meus … verba delictorum meorum*). Always
resolve the Vulgate number and verify by the opening words.
